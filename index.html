I work on machine learning for graphs and higher-order networks. I am currently a Research Engineer at Télécom Paris, Institut Polytechnique de Paris, working with Jhony H. Giraldo (Télécom Paris) and Fragkiskos Malliaros (CentraleSupélec). I will soon start my PhD at CentraleSupélec under their co-supervision, funded by the DIM AI4IDF, on generative modeling over product graphs for multi-domain (tensor) data.

My current work focuses on inferring latent hypergraphs of group interactions from data without relation labels and, in parallel, on faster Graph Fourier Transforms using low-rank representations and one- and two-step rank updates.

Before that, I was an AI Engineer at Hermes Trade, working on embedding models for retrieval. I received my MSc in Artificial Intelligence and Robotics from Sapienza University of Rome, where I worked on sheaf neural networks for directed hypergraphs with the RSTLess group (published at ICLR 2026) and on ground-to-aerial image matching for visual misinformation detection with the ALCOR Lab.

Publications

ICLR 2026

Directional Sheaf Hypergraph Networks: Unifying Learning on Directed and Undirected Hypergraphs

Emanuele Mule, Stefano Fiorini, Antonio Purificato, Federico Siciliano, Stefano Coniglio, Fabrizio Silvestri

International Conference on Learning Representations (ICLR), 2026

A sheaf-based framework for directed hypergraphs built on the Directed Sheaf Hypergraph Laplacian, a complex-valued operator that generalizes many existing graph and hypergraph Laplacians. Relative accuracy gains of 2–20% over 13 baselines on 7 real-world datasets.

WACV-W 2025

Enhancing Ground-to-Aerial Image Matching for Visual Misinformation Detection Using Semantic Segmentation

Emanuele Mule*, Matteo Pannacci*, Ali Ghasemi Goudarzi*, Francesco Pro, Lorenzo Papa, Luca Maiano, Irene Amerini

WACV 2025 Workshop on AI for Multimedia Forensics & Disinformation Detection (AI4MFDD)

SAN-QUAD, a four-stream Siamese-like architecture that matches ground-view images to satellite imagery using semantic segmentation of both views. Improvements of up to 9.8% over prior methods across fields of view on CVUSA.
